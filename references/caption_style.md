# Caption voice guide

**Rule: never mention a school-fee discount or percentage-off in captions**
(e.g. no "School Fee Discounted 40%", "50% Off School Fee"), even if the
matching poster shows one. Discounts stay poster-only; captions should not
advertise them. When building a `benefit_groups` list, leave discount facts out.

Two caption lengths, each with a distinct voice. Both are generated by
`scripts/generate_captions.py` from a config file (see
`examples/caption_config.json`) — the config holds facts (country, phone,
program duration, industries, discount, etc.), the script's phrase banks
hold the wording/voice, so re-running produces new combinations instead of
the same caption every time.

## Short captions
Casual, single-breath, built for a WhatsApp status or Instagram Story caption.
Modeled on Josh's own examples:

> "Anybody Wants to Come #SINGAPORE? study +Internship with SALARY. NO AGENCY FEE PM ME FOR VISA PROCESS"
> "One year Singapore visa provided for passport holders no agency fee Interested can DM me"
> "Welcome to exciting job opportunities in Singapore with on-the-job training and a salary! No agency fees. Interested candidates can message me directly."

Traits:
- 2-4 short fragments strung together, not a polished multi-sentence paragraph.
- Almost always ends on the call-to-action (PM/DM/WhatsApp me).
- Casual capitalization — SALARY, NO AGENCY FEE, PM ME are shouted for emphasis;
  everything else is normal case. Punctuation between fragments is loose (periods
  optional, run-ons okay).
- Every short caption ends with a small hashtag set (mandatory, not
  occasional): the destination tag plus 2-3 more picked from a pool of
  casual, lowercase tags — `#student`, `#studentlife`, `#studyabroad`,
  `#workabroad`, `#livingabroad`, `#paidinternship`, `#internship`,
  `#studyandwork`, `#abroadlife`, `#globalcareer`, `#studentraveler`,
  `#careerabroad` (see `SHORT_HASHTAG_POOL` in `scripts/generate_captions.py`).
  They're shuffled together so the destination tag isn't always first, e.g.
  `#internship #singapore #globalcareer`.
- No agency fee is almost always mentioned — it's the biggest trust signal.

## Long captions
Structured, scannable, built for a Facebook/Instagram feed post. Modeled on several
of Josh's own posts, e.g.:

```
🇸🇬 STUDY & WORK IN SINGAPORE | NO AGENT FEES
Looking for an opportunity to gain international experience?
✔️ 6 Months Study
✔️ 6 Months Paid On-the-Job Training
✔️ International Diploma
✔️ Attractive OJT Salary
✔️ Limited Intake
Available industries:
🔹 Retail
🔹 Office
🔹 F&B
🔹 Customer Service
🔹 Marketing & Sales
📩 PM for more details.
📱 WhatsApp: +65 92450213
```

```
🎓 Want to study in Singapore? 🇸🇬
Applications are now open for our Study & Paid Internship Program!
✔️ International Diploma
✔️ Paid Internship
✔️ No Agent Fee
✔️ Affordable Tuition Fee
✔️ Installment Payment Available
🌏 Build your future while gaining international work experience.
📩 Message us today!
📱 WhatsApp: +65 92450213
```

```
🚀 Ready for an international career?
Study and intern in Singapore with our 1-Year Program.
✔️ 6 Months Study
✔️ 6 Months Paid Internship
✔️ International Diploma
✔️ Flexible Payment Options
✔️ No Agent Fee
📩 Reserve your slot today!
📱 WhatsApp: +65 92450213
```

Traits:
- Opening line prefers an eye-catching mixed-case hook over a shouted ALL-CAPS
  banner: an emoji/flag lead-in plus a punchy statement or direct question
  ("Study + Earn in Singapore 🇸🇬", "🔥 Want to Study in Singapore and Get a Paid
  Internship?", "✨ Your Singapore Study + Internship Journey Starts Here 🇸🇬",
  "💼 Get Paid While You Study in Singapore 🇸🇬"). A couple of bolder ALL-CAPS lines
  ("STUDY + PAID INTERNSHIP IN SINGAPORE") are kept in the mix for variety, but
  mixed-case + emoji is now the default lean — it reads as more scroll-stopping.
- A one-line subtext/hook right under it — a question, a short mantra ("Study. Learn.
  Gain Experience."), or an announcement ("Applications are now open for...").
- A ✔️ checklist of program facts. Not every fact appears in every post — usually
  4-5 of the available facts, not the full list. The same fact can be worded several
  ways ("No Agent Fee" / "No Agent Fees" / "No Agency Fee", "6 Months Paid
  On-the-Job Training" / "6 Months Paid OJT" / "Paid Internship") — see
  `benefit_groups` in `examples/caption_config.json`, where each fact is a list of
  interchangeable phrasings and the generator picks one per caption.
- A 🔹 bulleted industries list under "Available industries:" — optional, shown on
  roughly half of posts, not every one.
- An occasional extra line before the CTA, e.g. "🌏 Build your future while gaining
  international work experience."
- Closing CTA block, always last: 📩 line (PM/DM/message — "me" or "us" both appear)
  then 📱 WhatsApp number.
- Facts (durations, industries, discount, phone) always come from the config —
  never invented.

## Regenerating captions
```bash
python3 scripts/generate_captions.py --config <config.json> --short 8 --long 4 --out captions.md
```
- `--short` / `--long` set how many of each to produce.
- `--seed <int>` makes a batch reproducible; omit it for a fresh random mix each run.
- Copy `examples/caption_config.json` and edit the facts (country, phone,
  `benefit_groups`, industries) for a new cohort/intake, then regenerate. Prefer
  `benefit_groups` (a list of phrasing lists, one list per fact) over a flat
  `benefits` list where possible — it's what gives long captions their variety.
